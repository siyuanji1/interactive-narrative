IMT 561 Team Harry and Adam Students & ChatGPT: A Global Perspective
A Truly Global Survey
In 2023–2024, researchers at the University of Ljubljana partnered with over 200 institutions worldwide to ask: How do university students really feel about ChatGPT?

The result: 23,218 responses from 149 countries — one of the largest cross-cultural studies on AI in education ever conducted. Each bubble is a country; size reflects response count.

Hover over a bubble to see the country and number of respondents.
Who Took the Survey?
The survey reached students across all levels of higher education and every major discipline.

Gender: Women made up 58% of respondents, men 40%, with 2% identifying as other or preferring not to say.

Study level: 82% were undergraduates — the population most exposed to academic integrity policy decisions around AI.

Field: Social sciences dominated (40%), followed by applied sciences (34%), giving a rich view across quantitative and qualitative disciplines.
ChatGPT Has Reached the Classroom
Nearly 69% of respondents had used ChatGPT at least once — a striking figure given that the tool was barely a year old when many were surveyed.

Among users, the majority use it "rarely" or "occasionally." Fewer than 4% describe themselves as extensive users.

This tells us ChatGPT has achieved mass awareness, but not yet deep daily integration in most students' academic routines.

Applied Sciences students lead adoption at 76.5% — computer science, engineering, and medicine students are building with or studying AI directly.

The social sciences, arts, and natural sciences sit close together at 68–70%, suggesting that curiosity about ChatGPT cuts across nearly every field.

What Are Students Using It For?
Students reach for ChatGPT most often for brainstorming and academic writing — tasks that benefit from an always-available thinking partner.

Summarizing and research assistance are also popular, reflecting ChatGPT's strength at distilling information quickly.

Coding assistance, math help, and creative writing rank lowest — outside computer science, technical use is still limited.

Scores on a 1–5 scale (Never → Always).
Students Are Worried About Ethics
Students aren't naive about the risks. Agreement sits above neutral that ChatGPT might mislead with inaccurate information, encourage plagiarism, and encourage cheating.

Fears about replacing formal education or increasing social isolation score comparatively lower — students are more concerned about day-to-day academic integrity than existential risks.

Bars right of center = above-neutral agreement; left = disagreement.
But They See Real Academic Value
Despite ethical concerns, students broadly believe ChatGPT can improve their education.

General knowledge and access to sources score highest — students see ChatGPT as an always-on encyclopedia and research assistant.

Personalized education and class engagement score lowest, suggesting skepticism that AI can replace the interactive, social classroom.

All items sit above neutral — the signal is cautious optimism.
Satisfied — But Not a Replacement
Students are most satisfied with ChatGPT as a practical tool: they rate it highly for assistance quality and information quality.

However, the idea that ChatGPT is easier than interacting with professors or colleagues scores well below neutral — students don't see it as a social substitute.

The message for AI policy: students want ChatGPT as a supplement, not a replacement for human academic relationships.
A Tool That Will Reshape Work
Students globally believe ChatGPT will change the nature of jobs, increase demand for AI skills, and facilitate remote work.

The most agreed-upon item: workers will need to acquire new skills. Students are under no illusion that the labor market is static.

Notably, "reduce the number of jobs" scores nearest to neutral — students see ChatGPT as transforming work, not eliminating it outright.
How It Feels to Use ChatGPT
The dominant emotion is curiosity — the single highest-scoring feeling — followed by calm and happiness.

Negative emotions like sadness, shame, and anger sit near the "Never" end of the scale. While students hold ethical concerns intellectually, the day-to-day experience is largely positive and engaging.

This emotional openness may partly explain rapid adoption: students approach ChatGPT with curiosity, not fear.
About This Project

This interactive article visualizes data from the Global ChatGPT Student Survey (CovidSocLab / University of Ljubljana, 2023–2024), a 174-question study spanning 149 countries and 23,218 higher education students.

The survey covers demographics, usage patterns, capabilities perceptions, ethical concerns, satisfaction, learning outcomes, labor market views, and emotional responses — a comprehensive snapshot of how the next generation thinks about generative AI.
