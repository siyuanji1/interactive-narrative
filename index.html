What Are Students Using AI For?
It's not just whether students use AI — it's what they use it for.

The spider chart shows five AI tasks on each axis. The further a shape extends toward an axis, the more that field relies on AI for that task.

The gray shape is the overall average across all 23,000+ students. Check individual fields below to see how each one compares:

  • Applied Sciences spikes sharply toward Coding — engineers and CS students use AI as a programming tool far more than any other group.
  • Arts & Humanities extend toward Writing and Brainstorming — using AI as a creative and drafting partner.
  • Social Sciences and Natural Sciences both lean toward Research and Summarizing.

More AI, more curiosity
As students use ChatGPT more, curiosity and hope rise steadily. Anxiety? It barely moves. Use the buttons to explore how this pattern shifts across different academic fields.
But not every field feels the same
Zoom in by field — Applied Sciences students are the most curious and least anxious. Arts & Humanities students show the highest anxiety and boredom rates. Hover over a line to highlight it.
AI helps you work — but does it help you grow?
Students feel AI boosts productivity: study efficiency, assignment quality, and meeting deadlines all score above 3.5. But critical thinking and creativity sit below 3.2. AI is helping students work faster — but the skills that define long-term career value are growing more slowly.
Job confidence gap
Each field has two dots — low AI users on the left, high AI users on the right. The line connecting them reveals the story.

Applied Sciences students who use AI heavily report much higher job confidence. Arts & Humanities students show the opposite — heavier AI use is associated with lower job confidence.

More AI use isn't automatically a confidence boost. The effect depends deeply on your field.
