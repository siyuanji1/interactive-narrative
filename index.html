Students & ChatGPT
How are students around the world really using AI?

We analyzed a global survey of 23,218 students across 149 countries to find out — exploring how ChatGPT adoption varies by field, and what it means for emotions, learning, and career confidence.

Scroll down to explore →
Does Your Major Predict Your Use?
Nearly 7 in 10 students across all fields have used ChatGPT — but the gap between fields tells a deeper story.

Applied Sciences leads at 76.5%. Engineers, CS, and pre-med students have the highest adoption rate — they were among the earliest and most consistent adopters across the survey.

Social Sciences, Arts & Humanities, and Natural Sciences all cluster between 68–70%. The spread is smaller than most would expect: the pull of AI isn't discipline-specific — it's universal.

Each card below represents 5 students. Watch them sort as you scroll — the length of each row shows the scale of adoption in each field.
What Are Students Using AI For?
It's not just whether students use AI — it's what they use it for.

The spider chart shows five AI tasks on each axis. The further a shape extends toward an axis, the more that field relies on AI for that task.

The gray shape is the overall average across all 23,000+ students.

More AI, more curiosity
You'd expect more AI use to mean more anxiety. The data disagrees.

Curiosity climbs +30% from rarely to extensively. Anxiety? It actually drops by the end. The gap between the two keeps widening — the more students use AI, the more curious they become, not more worried.

But this is the average across all students. Arts & Humanities students tell a strikingly different story — use the buttons below to see how their pattern diverges from the rest.
AI helps you work — but does it help you grow?
Students feel AI boosts productivity: study efficiency, assignment quality, and meeting deadlines all score above 3.5. But critical thinking and creativity sit below 3.2. AI is helping students work faster — but the skills that define long-term career value are growing more slowly.
Job confidence gap
Each field has two columns — Low AI users on the left, High AI users on the right. The dot marks the mean; the bar shows the interquartile range (Q1–Q3) — where the middle 50% of students fall.

Applied Sciences students who use AI heavily report meaningfully higher confidence (mean rises from 3.1 → 3.6). Arts & Humanities shows the opposite — heavier AI use is associated with lower job confidence (2.4 → 2.1).

More AI use isn't automatically a confidence boost. The effect depends deeply on your field.
